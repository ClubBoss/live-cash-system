## Smash Live Cash

### Best role

Advanced cash-strategy backbone.

### Strongest blocks

- effective stack and SPR translation;
- deep-stack architecture;
- OOP range protection;
- range shape that begins preflop and persists;
- 3-bet pots;
- multiway ownership and delayed aggression;
- active defence against heavy-check or tiny-bet strategies;
- later-street ownership and bluff-supply reconstruction.

### Main strengths

1. Most directly relevant source for the learner's MTT-to-cash transition.
2. Strongest received coverage of deep stacks, OOP discomfort and 3-bet-pot structure.
3. Strong advanced connection between preflop range shape and postflop execution.
4. Good treatment of multiway, which is critical in live cash and weakly covered elsewhere.
5. Produces robust mechanisms rather than only hand-level outputs.

### Main weaknesses

1. Dense and less beginner-friendly.
2. Requires substantial compression before table use.
3. Exact visual frequencies and charts are sometimes needed for narrow claims.
4. Can tempt the learner into overcomplicated reasoning if taught before simpler prerequisites.
5. Does not directly deliver the project's final compact anchor system.

### Project verdict

`HIGHEST_STRATEGIC_DEPTH / REQUIRES_PEDAGOGICAL_COMPRESSION`

Current project score: `9.1/10` as an evidence source, `7.2/10` as a standalone learner route.

## From the Ground Up

### Best role

Foundational sequence and conceptual bridge from basic cash principles to structured postflop reasoning.

### Strongest blocks

- equity versus EV;
- position and realisation;
- opening, limping and blind-call logic;
- polar, linear and mixed preflop strategies;
- range bet versus selective bet;
- protected calls and flop raises;
- combos and blockers;
- bet sizing families;
- pyramidal defence;
- river value/bluff audits;
- basic exploitative folds and calls.

### Main strengths

1. Best complete foundation among received full courses.
2. Strong course order and prerequisite flow.
3. Explains difficult concepts with relatively low cognitive load.
4. Covers preflop and postflop broadly enough to create a coherent mental model.
5. Very useful for diagnostics and micro-lessons.
6. Independent support for non-results-oriented study and prediction-before-feedback.

### Main weaknesses

1. Limited depth-specific treatment beyond the default 100bb frame.
2. Multiway and straddle coverage are insufficient for the target live environment.
3. Supplied charts cannot be admitted as exact anchors because assumptions and percentages are incomplete or inconsistent.
4. Some numerical bands and 50/50 language are pedagogical rather than exact.
5. Less advanced than Smash in difficult OOP and deep-stack trees.

### Project verdict

`BEST_COMPLETE_FOUNDATION / NOT_SUFFICIENT_FOR_DEEP_LIVE_CASH_ALONE`

Current project score: `8.6/10` as an evidence source, `8.4/10` as a standalone learner route.

## Cash Injection

### Best role

Exploit overlay and branch-classification system.

### Strongest blocks

- origin-range width;
- filtered versus unfiltered ranges;
- surviving value and air;
- small-size elasticity;
- overfold and underbluff branch splits;
- protected check-back and check branches;
- turn probes and float bets;
- small river probes;
- 3-bet-pot click-back branches;
- exploit falsifiers and baseline-return rules.

### Main strengths

1. Most directly practical exploit methodology of the received courses.
2. Excellent at showing that one opponent may overfold one branch and underbluff another.
3. Strengthens range ancestry and river audit with human-error models.
4. Generates useful field missions and falsifiable hypotheses.
5. Very compatible with an adaptive course because each exploit can be a conditional overlay.

### Main weaknesses

1. Population magnitudes are not independently demonstrated for Batumi live games.
2. Aggressive headline recommendations can be dangerous when applied literally.
3. Narrower curriculum breadth than Smash or FTGU.
4. Less useful as the primary baseline course.
5. Requires strict separation of mechanism from pool claim.

### Project verdict

`BEST_EXPLOIT_LAYER / MUST_REMAIN_EVIDENCE_GATED`

Current project score: `8.7/10` as an exploit source, `6.5/10` as a standalone complete course.

## Carrot Poker Grade 1 — received material

### Best role

Pedagogical operating system for postflop EV, range construction and interactive assessment.

### Strongest blocks

- full-tree EV comparison;
- open-action versus end-of-action reasoning;
- required pot share versus realised EV;
- mandatory, optional and prohibited value/bluff categories;
- urgency and check EV;
- favourable, neutral and unfavourable range worlds;
- polar versus condensed ranges;
- range advantage versus nut advantage;
- frequency and size as separate outputs;
- turn favourability after filtering;
- float-bet linearisation repair;
- river texture and range geography;
- threshold-based assessment design.

### Main strengths

1. Best pedagogy among received sources.
2. Best terminology for interactive decisions and misconception repair.
3. Converts abstract strategy into stable learner-facing selectors.
4. Strongest received source for assessment architecture.
5. Excellent fit for adaptive micro-lessons, prediction and confidence scoring.
6. Strong river and postflop construction framework.
7. Adds explanation quality without increasing the candidate count.

### Main weaknesses

1. Grade 1 is incomplete until Lecture 10 arrives.
2. Received material is mainly postflop and conceptual; it does not yet close preflop, squeeze or multiway gaps.
3. Deep-stack boundaries remain open.
4. Exact solver cells and visual outputs remain claim-driven.
5. Grades 2–3 may materially add boundaries or context not visible yet.

### Project verdict

`BEST_PEDAGOGICAL_AND_ASSESSMENT_LAYER / SOURCE_PHASE_INCOMPLETE`

Current score for received Grade 1 material: `9.2/10` as pedagogy, `8.5/10` as strategic evidence, `7.4/10` as a standalone incomplete route.
