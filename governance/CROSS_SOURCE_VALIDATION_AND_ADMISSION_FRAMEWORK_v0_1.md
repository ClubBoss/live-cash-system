# General Live Cash — Cross-Source Validation and Admission Framework v0.1

Status: `GOVERNANCE_ACTIVE`

Purpose: determine how mechanisms discovered in Smash Live Cash, Carrot Poker, Cash Injection, From the Ground Up, independent analysis and field evidence become original admitted Playbook rules.

## Source roles

### Smash Live Cash

Primary role:

- advanced live-cash mechanisms;
- deep-stack and solver-informed structures;
- node-locking and branch-specific exploits;
- multiway and 3-bet-pot examples.

### Carrot Poker Grades

Primary role:

- mechanism explanation;
- systematic theory progression;
- range construction and exploit logic;
- clarification of why rules work.

### Cash Injection

Primary role:

- practical exploit hypotheses;
- population-oriented adjustments;
- identification of common human mistakes.

### From the Ground Up Cash

Primary role:

- foundations;
- accessible defaults;
- simplification and sequencing;
- checking whether an advanced rule is teachable without hidden prerequisites.

### Independent analysis

Primary role:

- compress overlapping mechanisms;
- identify scope and exceptions;
- create original terminology, examples and drills;
- detect contradictions.

### Field evidence

Primary role:

- prioritize mechanisms;
- validate table cues and transfer;
- calibrate environment overlays;
- reveal misuse and missing exceptions.

Field evidence does not prove universal theory.

# Types of source relationship

## `SR-01 — Direct agreement`

Sources teach materially the same mechanism within the same scope.

Action:

- confidence increases;
- original compression can proceed.

## `SR-02 — Different abstraction level`

One source gives a simple default; another gives detailed exceptions.

Action:

- preserve default in core;
- place exception in supporting layer.

## `SR-03 — Different stack depth`

Advice differs because one source assumes 100bb and another 200bb+.

Action:

- do not call it contradiction;
- tag depth scope explicitly.

## `SR-04 — Different population model`

One source assumes equilibrium defence; another assumes live under-defence.

Action:

- separate baseline from exploit overlay.

## `SR-05 — Different rake or game structure`

Advice differs because of rake, ante, straddle, player count or open size.

Action:

- move variation into environment-sensitive branch.

## `SR-06 — Simplification tradeoff`

A source recommends a simple executable strategy that differs from maximum-EV mixing.

Action:

- compare EV cost, implementation risk and learning value;
- possibly admit simplification as a product default.

## `SR-07 — Genuine contradiction`

Same node, assumptions and objective produce incompatible recommendations.

Action:

- block admission;
- inspect exact ranges, sizes and evidence;
- resolve or represent uncertainty explicitly.

# Validation questions

For every candidate ask:

1. Is the trigger observable at the table?
2. Does the rule specify its stack-depth scope?
3. Does it distinguish baseline from exploit?
4. Does it survive a counterexample?
5. Does another source support the mechanism?
6. Is disagreement explained by assumptions?
7. Can the rule be executed without exact unavailable frequencies?
8. Does it reduce cognitive load?
9. Can misuse be diagnosed?
10. Can it be taught with original examples?

# Admission classes

## `A0 — Research note`

Interesting source observation; no compact rule yet.

## `A1 — Candidate`

Trigger, default and reason exist.

## `A2 — Cross-source supported`

At least one independent source supports the same mechanism or clarifies scope.

## `A3 — Drill validated`

Original variants demonstrate that learner can apply default and exception.

## `A4 — Field-test pending`

Ready for real-session use with tracked evidence.

## `A5 — Admitted`

Compact, non-duplicative, source-pure and field-usable.

## `A6 — Field validated`

Observed to transfer correctly in real play or accurate post-session reconstruction.

## `AX — Rejected or superseded`

Failed scope, duplicate, misleading or unsupported.

# Intellectual-property purity gate

A commercial or Sharky-ready rule must pass all conditions:

1. Text written independently from scratch.
2. No course transcript or close paraphrase exposed.
3. No proprietary chart, screenshot or exported range reused.
4. No course-specific hand sequence required for understanding.
5. Original examples or independently generated solver work used.
6. Original taxonomy and drill design.
7. Source is cited internally for provenance but not republished.
8. Product does not substitute for access to the original course.

# Simplification gate

A rule may be simpler than source equilibrium when:

- exact mixing is difficult to implement;
- EV loss is believed small or environment exploit offsets it;
- the simplification preserves range shape;
- the learner can recognize exceptions;
- simplification does not create a transparent catastrophic leak.

Every simplification should state:

- what complexity was removed;
- likely cost;
- when it fails;
- whether it is training-only or table-ready.

# Conflict-resolution workflow

1. Freeze candidate promotion.
2. Normalize assumptions:
   - positions;
   - depth;
   - rake/ante;
   - player count;
   - sizes;
   - baseline or exploit.
3. Identify whether conflict remains.
4. Inspect source fidelity and visual dependencies.
5. Build an independent test or solver comparison if required.
6. Admit one scoped branch, both scoped branches or neither.
7. Record the decision.

# Field evidence interaction

Field evidence may change:

- learning priority;
- exploit weighting;
- environment profile;
- exception prominence;
- table cue wording.

It should not silently change:

- source claim;
- general theoretical mechanism;
- exact range chart;
- unresolved transcript text.

# Initial validation order

When Carrot and FTGU are ingested, validate in this order:

1. effective stack and protected flats;
2. squeeze candidate purification;
3. blind identity and range source;
4. deep OOP protected checking;
5. value-first aggression and bluff jobs;
6. size-to-range-shape interpretation;
7. 3-bet range ancestry;
8. multiway shared defence;
9. blocker ancestry and river value counting;
10. short-stack overlay.

This order prioritizes high-frequency, transferable mechanisms.
