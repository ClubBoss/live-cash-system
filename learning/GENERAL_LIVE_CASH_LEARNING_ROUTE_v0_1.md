# General Live Cash — Learning Route v0.1

Status: `CURRICULUM_ARCHITECTURE`

Purpose: define a compact progression from node recognition to live-session transfer without forcing the learner to memorize a large chart library.

## Curriculum principles

1. Mechanism before frequency.
2. Node recognition before action selection.
3. Effective depth before hand strength.
4. Range construction before blockers.
5. Heads-up structure before multiway complexity.
6. Robust baseline before exploit.
7. One new mechanism per micro-session.
8. Error-linked repetition rather than lesson-linked repetition.
9. Environment profiles are overlays, not the core curriculum.
10. Exact charts are anchors, not the learning system itself.

## Depth weighting for the initial general product

Initial study emphasis:

- around 100bb: 50–60%;
- 125–200bb: 30–40%;
- 200bb+: 10–15%;
- 30–70bb overlay: mandatory but currently source-incomplete;
- sub-30bb classical short-stack play: separate specialist module if field evidence justifies it.

These are curriculum design weights, not solver frequencies.

## Stage 11 — Live-session transfer

### Before session

- two table cues;
- one fragile mechanism;
- one environment hypothesis to observe, not assume.

### During session

Record only high-value uncertain nodes. Avoid full-hand diary overload.

### After session

- classify node;
- diagnose misconception;
- map heuristic;
- create one repair drill;
- schedule delayed variant.

### Gate

A mechanism becomes `FIELD_VALIDATED` only after correct real-hand recognition or accurate post-session reconstruction.

# Suggested initial 20-session sequence

Each session is approximately 15–25 minutes.

1. Effective stack and pairwise depth.
2. Straddle units and SPR.
3. BB versus SB range identity.
4. Squeeze categories and protected flats.
5. Flop call filters the turn range.
6. Deep OOP protected check-call.
7. Value threshold before bluffing.
8. Three bluff jobs.
9. Overbet ownership and counterexamples.
10. Small versus large c-bet defence.
11. Vulnerable top-pair raises.
12. Turn-lead families.
13. 3-bet range shape from preflop.
14. Wide-range compensation failure.
15. Strong bet branch versus weak check branch.
16. Bluff ancestry and later-street folds.
17. Multiway shared defence.
18. Sandwich, closing action and nut ownership.
19. Multiway backup-equity bluffs and fast-play value.
20. River value count and blocker audit.

After Session 20, the route becomes adaptive rather than fixed.

# Graduation standard for the first route

The learner should be able to:

1. identify node and depth quickly;
2. use no more than a few anchor ranges;
3. explain range shape behind common sizes;
4. update ranges after actions;
5. construct aggression from value and bluff jobs;
6. avoid heads-up logic in multiway pots;
7. trace river bluffs backward;
8. distinguish baseline from exploit;
9. diagnose own recurring misconception;
10. convert a session hand into a targeted repair.
