# Source-Faithful Record

The lesson identifies three-bet-pot flops where the aggressor's range advantage is so concentrated that being out of position does not prevent very high-frequency betting. Dry high-card and paired boards preserve the preflop advantage because the three-bettor retains premium pairs and strong broadways at much higher density than the caller.

A small size is paired with the high-frequency strategy because the range contains many medium-strength protection bets and the pot is already enlarged. The source warns that range betting the flop does not justify range betting again on the turn. After the caller folds the weakest hands, the caller's range becomes stronger while the aggressor still retains all flop air; turn strategy must therefore become more selective.

# Explicit Instructor Mechanisms

- Bet very frequently when the flop preserves a concentrated premium-range advantage.
- Dry, paired and high-card boards often act like a transparent layer over the preflop ranges.
- Use a small size when betting many medium-strength and protection hands.
- Coordinated middling boards equalise ranges and remove the range-bet license.
- After a range bet and call, recalculate both ranges; the defender filtered air while the bettor did not.
- Do not copy a flop range bet mechanically onto the turn.
