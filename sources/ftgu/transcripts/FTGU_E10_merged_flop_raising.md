## [00:00] Polar versus merged raising

The lesson contrasts the prior episode's polar raising strategy with merged raising. The central rule is stated as:

- respond more polar against a polar betting range;
- respond more merged against a merged betting range.

A merged betting range contains strong hands, weak hands and much of the medium-strength region between them.

## [04:40] Board texture filters preflop ranges

A low disconnected flop is used to challenge the assumption that every low board belongs to the big blind. Peter says board texture must be applied to the preflop ranges rather than read in isolation. The button can retain a large range advantage because the big blind folded many weak low-card combinations before the flop and often lacks the strongest overpairs.

## [06:30] Why a small range bet invites merged raises

When the in-position player bets a small size with nearly the whole range, many overcards and marginal showdown-value hands receive cheap equity realisation. The defender cannot answer only with sets and bluffs; some vulnerable one-pair hands must raise to deny equity.

The bettor also cannot fold most of the range after betting almost everything. That required continuation creates thin-value opportunities for the raiser.

## [08:45] Value and protection coexist

Medium-strength hands may raise for both thin value and protection. The balance between the two depends on:

- how often the hand is currently ahead;
- how vulnerable it is to the bettor's folding hands;
- how many worse hands can continue to the raise;
- how selective or range-wide the original bet was.

Protection is not defined as "raising draws". It is the value gained by making live overcards and other folding hands surrender equity.

## [12:15] Trigger discipline

The merged raise is conditional. It is attractive when the original bet is small and range-wide. Against a selective, polar range, the same medium-strength raise can run into too much strong value and deny too little equity.

## [15:45] A merged range still includes polar hands

Merged does not mean removing sets or bluffs. It means retaining the polar ends and adding appropriate middle-strength hands. Candidate selection still considers showdown value, vulnerability, improvement potential and redraws.
