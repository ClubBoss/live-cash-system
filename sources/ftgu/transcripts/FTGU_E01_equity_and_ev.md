# Detailed Source-Faithful Record

## [00:00] Course scope

The course is introduced as a foundation for no-limit hold'em with a focus on six-max cash. Peter says many concepts can transfer to other player counts, stakes and tournament formats, but the primary teaching frame is six-max cash.

## [01:50] Equity

Equity is defined as the percentage of the pot a hand expects to win at showdown if all remaining cards are seen. It depends on the hand, opposing range, board and number of opponents.

The lesson immediately limits this concept: equity can be surrendered before showdown. A hand may have substantial equity but realise none of it after folding to pressure.

## [05:00] Expected value

EV is described as the average monetary outcome of an action. Exact EV is usually unknown in play, so the practical task is to estimate which available action has the highest EV under the assumptions being made.

A river value-bet example introduces branches of an EV tree: the call branch and the fold branch are weighted by their frequencies, then combined. The result is an average, not an amount that must occur in one hand.

## [09:40] When equity and EV align

Equity becomes more closely related to EV when few meaningful decisions remain: all-in situations, the river, or any node where the next decision ends the hand. With no future betting leverage or equity denial, price and equity dominate.

## [11:10] When equity and EV diverge

When many decisions and a large amount of stack remain, future folds, implied odds and later pressure can dominate current equity. A weak draw can continue with poor immediate equity if the future decision tree is favourable.

Peter explicitly links this to stack-to-pot ratio and says the course will normally assume 100 big blinds unless otherwise stated.

## [12:40] Implied odds

Implied odds are framed as the ratio between the current investment and the potential future return, not merely the chance of winning a large pot. A pocket-pair call against a tight early-position range is used to show how low current equity can coexist with a profitable call when the price is small and the upside of making a set is large.

## [15:15] Pot odds and required equity

An all-in example shows that a call does not need to win more than half the time. When risking one unit to win two, the break-even equity is about one third. A hand with 38% equity can therefore be a profitable call despite losing most individual runouts.

# Explicit Instructor Mechanisms

- Equity is not EV.
- Equity matters most when the action tree is nearly finished.
- Future leverage and implied odds can make a low-equity action profitable.
- Range-based reasoning is more realistic than assigning one exact hand.
- Maximise estimated EV, not current equity or win frequency.
- Pot odds determine required equity; the threshold is not automatically 50%.
