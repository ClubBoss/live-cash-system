# Detailed Source-Faithful Record

## [01:20] Four postflop sizing families

The lesson introduces four approximate categories:

- block: roughly 25-40% pot;
- modest: roughly half pot to about two thirds;
- big: a larger, more selective sizing around three quarters to pot, with overlap at the boundary;
- overbet: above pot, with examples around 125-150% and potentially larger.

The categories are explicitly presented as simplifications along a sliding scale.

## [03:00] Size and range shape

Smaller bets can support more merged ranges. Larger bets generally become more polar. The overbet is connected to a strong uncapped-versus-capped relationship: the bettor holds the strongest hands more credibly while the caller lacks them.

## [05:00] Block bet

A river block-bet example uses a marginal made hand that can receive calls from worse, avoid facing a larger bet after checking and lose less against better hands. Peter also notes that an aggressive opponent's delayed-bluff tendency can make check-call preferable.

## [08:30] Modest sizing

The modest bet is described as versatile. It can combine value, bluffing and equity denial when the betting range contains several hand functions and does not justify a sharply polar size.

## [11:45] Big bet

The big bet supports a more selective polar range, applying pressure while limiting the extra loss when the opponent can still retain nutted hands.

## [14:00] Overbet

The overbet maximises pressure when the bettor is uncapped and the opponent is capped. It is paired with very strong value and bluffs that cannot profitably reach showdown, subject to how often the opponent can still hold the nuts.

# Explicit Instructor Mechanisms

- Bet size expresses range shape; smaller tends to merged, larger tends to polar.
- Thin value and control can justify a block size.
- A medium size is useful when several hand functions share one branch.
- Big bets are selective and polar.
- Overbets require a credible nut advantage and a capped opposing range.
- Blockers and removal effects can shift the preferred size by changing the opponent's call region.
