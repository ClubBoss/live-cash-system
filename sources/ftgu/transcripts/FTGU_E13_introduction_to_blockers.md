# Detailed Source-Faithful Record

## [00:00] Blockers as combo removal

A blocker is defined as a card in the player's hand that removes combinations from an opponent's possible range. The lesson builds directly on the prior combo episode.

## [02:30] Blockers are role-dependent

The same card can be positive or negative depending on the action and the target range. There is no universally "good blocker" independent of the line.

## [03:30] Bluffing

For a bluff, favourable removal usually blocks strong calls or value while avoiding cards that remove the opponent's folds. A flush-completing example shows why holding a high card of the completed suit can remove strong flushes from the opponent, but other runouts can make that same card undesirable if it removes missed draws intended to fold.

## [05:20] Bluff-catching

A bluff-catcher prefers to block value and unblock bluffs. A hand that removes the opponent's natural bluffing draws can become a poor call even when its raw rank appears unchanged.

## [09:30] Value betting

For value, the relevant removal often reverses again: the bettor usually wants to unblock worse hands that can call. A card that blocks those calls can reduce value-bet EV.

# Explicit Instructor Mechanisms

- Blockers are a direct consequence of combo removal.
- Blocker quality depends on the action: bluff, call or value bet.
- Bluff with cards that remove continues/value and preserve folds where possible.
- Bluff-catch with cards that remove value and preserve bluffs.
- Value bet with hands that preserve worse calls.
