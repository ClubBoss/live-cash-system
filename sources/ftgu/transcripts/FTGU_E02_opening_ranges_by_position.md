# Source-Faithful Record

The lesson treats charts as defaults whose logic matters more than memorisation. Opening profitability is constrained by rake, position, players remaining, equity realisation and how defensibly the range can respond to three-bets.

UTG is built mainly for value and field thinning. Suited aces receive special treatment because of nut potential and ace removal. HJ adds semi-steals: hands that can tolerate action but often prefer folds. CO adds more high-equity steals; BU adds lower-equity steals because only two players remain and position improves realisation. SB remains wide but is tightened relative to BU because all postflop play is out of position.

The ranges are deliberately adjustable. Tight/aggressive players behind remove fringe opens; passive or weak players behind justify widening. The instructor repeatedly warns against opening a range so wide that defending roughly half of it against pressure requires obviously losing continues.

# Explicit Instructor Mechanisms

- Rake removes marginal opens; lower-rake games justify wider ranges.
- Position widens opening ranges gradually rather than through a binary steal/non-steal switch.
- Opening reasons progress through value, field thinning, semi-steal, high-equity steal and low-equity steal.
- A default range should remain defensible against competent three-betting.
- Adjust the fringe of the range to players behind, not only to position labels.
- Equity denial and equity realisation are central to preflop range design.
