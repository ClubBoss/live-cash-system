# Detailed Source-Faithful Record

## [00:00] Balance play and study

Peter recommends variety rather than excessive volume in one activity. Play, study, hand review, discussion and breaks should reinforce each other. Too much play without feedback creates habits; too much solver or chart work creates information overload and misses profitable exploits.

## [05:15] Build concepts in a coherent hierarchy

The player's game is described as a building under permanent construction. Later concepts such as protection, merged ranges, balance and exploit depend on foundations such as equity, fold equity, value and bluffing.

A new idea should fit the existing framework rather than contradict it without explanation. Memorised instructions and isolated charts do not substitute for understanding.

## [07:20] Think independently

Imitating a strong player's action without its assumptions is criticised. The student should explain why a concept applies, identify conflicts with existing reasoning and ask how the new idea connects to the wider model.

## [09:20] Reject result-oriented review

A lost bluff is not automatically bad and a successful call is not automatically good. Review should compare the in-game reasoning with the available information and identify what was correct or incomplete in the process.

## [10:30] Active disagreement and feedback

The student should form an answer before receiving a coach's verdict, challenge explanations that appear inconsistent and use the discussion to refine a coherent model.

# Explicit Instructor Mechanisms

- Mix play, study, review and recovery rather than overloading one mode.
- Learn concepts in prerequisite order.
- Do not imitate actions without the underlying trigger and assumptions.
- Judge decisions by reasoning, not outcome.
- Predict and explain before receiving feedback.
- Treat disagreement as part of learning when it is reasoned and evidence-based.
