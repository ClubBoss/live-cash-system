## Dynamic isolation spots

Limped pots are treated as highly opponent- and table-dependent. The relevant variables include:

- number of limpers;
- their stack sizes;
- limp-fold, limp-call and limp-reraise tendencies;
- players behind and their squeeze frequency;
- whether the pot is likely to remain heads-up or become multiway.

Peter does not recommend one universal isolation chart for this lesson.

## Three-factor isolation framework

The decision to isolate, overlimp or fold is organised around:

1. **Frequent strength** — how often the hand flops a robust made hand or strong draw, rather than how spectacularly it performs on rare runouts.
2. **Fold equity** — how often the limper and remaining players can be made to fold preflop or shortly after the flop.
3. **Position** — a powerful EV amplifier when meaningful stack remains behind.

Frequent strength and fold equity are partly substitutable: a hand that often flops strong does not need as much fold equity; a weak hand may isolate profitably only when fold equity and position are excellent.

## Implied-odds hands

Small pairs and suited connected hands may prefer overlimping when:

- fold equity is poor;
- the pot will be multiway;
- a raise would face calls or squeezes too often;
- keeping the initial investment small materially improves implied odds.

## Isolation sizing

Isolation sizing is generally larger than a normal open because the raise is trying to discourage action and charge the limper. The size should be reduced when aggressive players behind can 3-bet frequently, because an oversized raise makes defending against the 3-bet harder. Out of position, a strong hand may use a larger size to punish opponents' improved realisation and implied odds.

# Explicit Instructor Mechanisms

- Limped-pot strategy is table-specific rather than chart-only.

- Preserve implied odds by minimising the initial investment when fold equity is poor.
- Size isolation raises according to position, expected calls and squeeze risk.
