# Detailed Source-Faithful Record

## The third reason to call: pot odds

In addition to value and implied odds, the big blind can call because the blind already invested creates a favourable price. A hand can be behind the opening range and still produce a higher EV by calling than by folding.

The correct comparison is not whether the call wins more often than it loses. It is whether the call loses less than the automatic loss of folding the big blind.

## Closing the action and opponent range

Big-blind defence improves when:

- the defender closes the action;
- the open size is small;
- the opening range is wide;
- the hand has enough playability to realise part of its raw equity.

The same weak hand performs materially worse against an early-position range because top-pair domination and strong-overpair density increase.

## Rake and player skill

Marginal calls can cross from profitable to unprofitable under high rake or poor postflop execution. Peter presents the numerical EV illustrations as approximations rather than exact calculations.

## Decision framing

The lesson explicitly rejects the intuition that a play must be "more good than bad" in isolation. Dead money and pot odds can make an action that loses most pots preferable to folding.

# Explicit Instructor Mechanisms
