# Source-Faithful Record

A turn probe is a lead by the preflop caller after the in-position preflop raiser checks back the flop. Under standard 100bb conditions, the missed c-bet often removes many nutted flop hands from the aggressor's range, creating an uncapped-versus-capped relationship for the caller.

The lesson uses two turn sizes. A modest probe contains thinner value, protection and lower-EV bluffs. A large overbet is more polar, using strong value that does not block the opponent's likely calls and higher-quality bluffs with suitable blockers and improvement potential.

The source also warns that a turn card can strengthen the flop-check range and reduce or remove the cap. The probe plan therefore begins with the exact checked range and the new card rather than a mechanical "missed c-bet means overbet" rule.

# Explicit Instructor Mechanisms

- Identify what strong hands the flop check-back removed before probing.
- Use a modest size for thin value, protection and selected low-EV bluffs.
- Use an overbet for uncapped value and higher-quality polar bluffs.
- Value overbets should preserve the opponent's strongest bluff-catchers where possible.
- Reassess whether the turn card has uncapped the checked range.
- Maintain a checking range; probing is not automatic.
