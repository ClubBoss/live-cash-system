# Detailed Source-Faithful Record

## Start from range advantage

The lesson uses button versus big blind to show that the preflop raiser often begins with stronger high-card and overpair density, while the big blind's discounted calling range contains more weak and medium hands. The board then determines how much of that initial advantage remains.

## Unselective small betting

On boards where the raiser retains a large and concentrated advantage, a small high-frequency bet can be used with nearly the whole range. Its jobs include:

- thin value;
- equity denial and protection;
- fold equity for weak hands;
- pot building with position and range advantage;
- simplification of the strategy.

A range bet is paired with a small size because many medium and weak hands share the branch.

## Selective betting

When the board connects better with the big blind, neutralises overpairs or gives the defender more two-pair/straight density, the raiser becomes more selective. The betting range shifts toward strong value and bluffs, while medium-strength showdown hands check more.

Peter treats selective versus unselective as a spectrum rather than a rigid binary.

## Board labels are insufficient alone

A board is not automatically "good for the big blind" because it is low or connected. The board must be applied to the actual preflop ranges and their missing premium combinations.

# Explicit Instructor Mechanisms

- C-bet frequency begins with range and nut advantage, not initiative alone.
- Large concentrated advantage supports a small high-frequency bet.
- Equalising boards require a more selective polar strategy.
- Small range bets combine thin value, protection and fold equity.
- Board texture must filter preflop range shape.
- Selective and unselective are endpoints on a continuum.
