# Detailed Source-Faithful Record

## Why small-blind flats are structurally weak

The small blind receives only a modest discount, remains out of position and does not close the action. A flat can be squeezed by the big blind, denying the caller all equity. The caller also enters postflop with a capped range after declining to 3-bet premium hands.

## Benefits of 3-betting

A 3-bet can:

- uncaps the range;
- isolate the opener;
- deny equity to the opener and big blind;
- avoid being squeezed;
- reduce how often rake is paid by winning preflop;
- create a lower-SPR pot where position matters somewhat less.

## Hand-class selection

The lesson distinguishes hands that benefit from larger pots and fold equity from hands that suffer after the opener's range is filtered.

Dominatable offsuit broadways can perform poorly as 3-bets because the caller's range becomes concentrated in stronger broadways. Small pairs may lack enough frequent strength to inflate the pot and are used only occasionally for coverage. Suited wheel aces benefit from blocker-driven fold equity and retain postflop playability.

## Conditional flats

The title is a simplification rather than a literal prohibition. Some flats can remain when the big blind is weak/passive or when a versatile hand benefits from keeping weaker ranges in. The table behind determines whether the flat survives.

# Explicit Instructor Mechanisms

- Small-blind calls are penalised by poor price, position, squeeze risk and cappedness.
- 3-betting can solve several structural disadvantages at once.
- Do not 3-bet a hand merely because flatting is unattractive; evaluate the called 3-bet branch.
- Dominated offsuit broadways lose value after range filtering.
- Blocker hands can gain fold equity without being unplayable when called.
- 3-bet-or-fold is a useful default, not an unconditional law.
