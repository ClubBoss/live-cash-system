# Source-Faithful Record

This episode occupies the middle ground between range checking and range betting. When a flop materially equalises the preflop ranges but does not fully reverse the advantage, the three-bettor should split the range.

The betting branch becomes more selective and polar: strongest value plus the most promising bluffs. Medium showdown value, weaker pairs and give-ups populate checks. The source stresses that high cards alone do not guarantee the aggressor owns the board; if both players share the nutted hands, the preflop premium advantage has been neutralised.

For practical simplicity, the lesson recommends avoiding an unnecessarily complex five-part strategy. When the first decision already splits bet and check, the check branch can often be simplified to calls and folds rather than also constructing a large check-raise system.

# Explicit Instructor Mechanisms

- Use a split strategy when range and positional advantage are moderate rather than extreme.
- Shared nutted hands indicate meaningful range equalisation.
- Bet a more polar range and check medium showdown value plus low-prospect air.
- Do not range bet merely because the board contains high cards.
- Avoid paying an automatic "extra blind" by betting every hand on equalising boards.
- Prefer a simpler protected check branch when additional check-raise complexity adds little practical value.
