## [00:00] Solve the whole response range through landmarks

The lecture moves to facing bets and introduces threshold mapping for:

- value raises;

The purpose is to locate Hero’s hand inside the response range rather than treating it as an isolated decision.

## [12:00] Range geography

“Range geography” means identifying the borders where one action stops and another becomes permissible. Useful landmarks include:

- worst value raise;
- strongest hand that still only calls;
- marginal call/fold point;
- strongest bluff-raise candidate;
- hands mixing among multiple actions.

Knowing these borders gives a more durable mental map than memorising one exact hand.

## [20:00] Compare call and raise EV

The lecture extends the Grade 1 mandatory/optional/prohibited classification to responses against bets. A hand can be:

- mandatory raise;
- optional raise/call;
- mandatory call;
- marginal call/fold;
- mandatory fold.

A raise can be driven by value, bluffing and denial. The source again rejects ease-of-play as a theoretical reason, though it may matter exploitatively.

## [30:00] Range bet versus selective bet

Facing a range-wide small bet is strategically different from facing the same size from a selective, more polarised range.

Against the range bet:

- the bettor retains many medium and weak hands;
- Hero can defend more widely;
- thinner value raises and merged raises become available;
- the calling threshold moves downward.

Against the selective bet:

- the bettor is more polarised;
- Hero raises less;
- more medium hands prefer call;
- the response range becomes more cautious.

## [44:00] Value beaters, bluff catchers and frail hands

The lecture classifies made hands facing aggression into:

- value beaters that defeat part of the opponent’s value range;
- bluff catchers that beat bluffs but not value;
- frail hands that may lose even to some bluffs.

This classification precedes blocker selection.

## [46:00] Initial bluff-catching system

Bluff-catcher continuation depends on:

- opponent sizing;
- world favourability;
- position;
- robustness or frailness;
- the expected bluff-to-value composition;
- blockers only after those factors.

The source explicitly warns against theory calls when a real opponent is meaningfully underbluffing.
