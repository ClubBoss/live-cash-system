## Question map

### Q1 — Turn-dependent frequency and sizing

Approximate section: `00:31–07:57`.

Key source claims:

- an ace turn can improve IP EV even when raw equity is not dominant because it removes some of BB's ace-x density while preserving IP's strong overpairs and top-end range;
- a turn connected to the flop's continue region can improve BB's relative range and nut position, lowering IP EV and suppressing overbets;
- blank turns preserve a large polarisation advantage and can support overbet-or-check strategies;
- sizing follows which value region wants to reopen the action, not the superficial strength of the turn card.

### Q2 — OOP turn probe construction

Approximate section: `07:57–15:10`.

Key source claims:

- probe frequency is very low in an unfavourable world even after IP checks back, because the check-back range remains protected;
- when OOP does probe, the range is narrow and polar, so the preferred size is large;
- a weaker hand can be a better bluff than a stronger hand when its bet EV is higher relative to its check EV;
- a bet can be worse than folding when the hand blocks folds, has weak nut potential and retains some check EV;
- raw equity is not the criterion for bluff quality.

### Q3 — Mandatory river betting

Approximate section: `15:10–19:49`.

Key source claims:

- ace-highs become mandatory bets when IP remains in a very favourable range state, fold equity exceeds the neutral benchmark and check EV is low;
- checking air in such a node is a severe river-blunder error;
- action sequence can make an apparently modest two-pair hand a clear value bet because stronger regions would often have invested earlier;
- relative hand strength must be reconstructed from the line.

### Q4 — Value tiers and sizing

Approximate section: `19:49–25:52`.

Key source claims:

- top value hands can choose very different sizes because blockers alter which calling region remains;
- a stronger absolute hand may size smaller when it blocks the opponent's principal calls;
- tier-one versus tier-two classification interacts with unblockers and the opponent's raise region;
- strong showdown hands that cannot profitably reopen the action remain checks IP.

### Q5 — OOP delayed c-bet and slow-play

Approximate section: `25:52–30:13`.

Key source claims:

- medium ace-high can be a thin value/denial bet rather than a bluff;
- strong hands may check because OOP retains check-raise access and can earn from the opponent's bluffs;
- a stronger hand may prefer betting when it unblocks calls and loses value from checks behind;
- speculative bluffs are selected partly by pair-draw implied odds and blockers to raises.

### Q6 — Urgency, Tier 3 and positive check EV

Approximate section: `30:13–36:47`.

Key source claims:

- urgency is high IP because checking closes the action and forfeits immediate investment opportunities;
- some strong hands still mix checks when they block calls or face an uncapped OOP range;
- Tier 3 hands often check IP because their investment ceiling is low and reopening the action exposes them to raises;
- a very weak hand may bluff because no stronger candidate has a better bet-versus-check EV difference;
- a weak hand's positive check EV can come mainly from delayed future fold equity rather than showdown value.

### Q7 — Robustness and frailness thresholds

Approximate section: `36:47–41:13`.

Key source claims:

- the robustness threshold is the made-hand call/fold boundary for hands deriving EV mainly from showdown value;
- the frailness threshold is the continue boundary for hands that rely on improving or bluffing later;
- Grade A/B/C bluff catchers are distinguished by blockers, redraw robustness and vulnerability to bluff improvement;
- suits must be evaluated explicitly; aggregate hand labels can hide large EV differences.

### Q8 — Triple-barrel bluff-catching and bluff-raising

Approximate section: `41:13–46:02`.

Key source claims:

- a bluff catcher loses EV when it blocks a high-density bluff card in the bettor's range;
- river bluff-raises should block value or calls and avoid blocking bet-folds;
- the bottom of Hero's range is not automatically the correct bluff-raise region;
- subtle suit differences can be explained by earlier-street bluff selection and which missed draws survive to the river;
- the source previews a Grade 3 `interference theorem` for river raising.

### Q9 — 3-bet-pot flop frequency and sizing

Approximate section: `46:02–52:19`.

Key source claims:

- range advantage primarily shapes betting frequency;
- nut advantage / relative polarisation primarily shapes sizing;
- paired boards can preserve or amplify the 3-bettor's overpair and top-end advantage;
- a range can have reasonable EV but still prefer checking when the opponent's range is more polar;
- very strong overpair regions can support larger bets even when overall range equity is not overwhelming.

### Q10 — Postflop raising and mixed actions

Approximate section: `52:19–57:36`.

Key source claims:

- a three-way fold/call/raise mix can occur at a frailness threshold when all actions are near zero EV;
- a medium pocket pair can raise as a thin/hybrid raise because fold equity and rare high-payoff improvement branches matter;
- a strong top-pair hand can raise even though some bluffs fold because it trades lower win frequency for larger win magnitude against continues;
- the instructor gives an explicit exploit suggestion to over-raise some range-bet nodes against humans, but this remains opponent/field gated;
- Grade 3 is announced as the next course, covering finer triple-barrel bluff selection and flop/turn raise construction.
