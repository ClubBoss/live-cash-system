## [00:00] Cold-call pot study framework

Nick studies HJ versus BTN at 200bb with an ante-like wide BTN flatting range. The purpose is to learn range-versus-range strategy rather than rely on one exact preflop chart.

## [01:55] Two-size OOP framework

The study uses a small and a medium/large size to reveal the shape of the OOP c-bet strategy. In-game sizes can deviate once the shape is understood.

## [03:50] High-card boards favour the opener

Boards with two or three high cards often favour HJ because HJ retains premium pairs and offsuit broadways that BTN sometimes 3-bets.

## [05:45] Low and mid boards favour the caller

Three-low-card and low monotone boards often favour BTN's pocket pairs, suited hands and set region, requiring HJ to check heavily.

## [07:40] Passive OOP strategy has information value

Checking deep OOP preserves SPR and lets HJ observe BTN's size and action before committing money.

## [09:35] Offsuit combinations drive board classification

The key preflop question is which offsuit top-pair hands BTN flats and which pocket pairs remain in the calling range. Q-4-2 and J-5-4 can therefore be poor HJ boards.

## [11:30] Why spread rainbow boards are difficult

BTN's suited hands retain many backdoors, making it unclear what HJ's bet is folding. Deep value thresholds also discourage blind linear betting.

## [13:25] Where HJ uses the big size

Three-high-card boards, flop-straight boards and top-pair-advantage boards support larger betting because HJ has premium value and BTN has forced continues.

## [15:20] Where HJ avoids the big size

Monotone, low and caller-connected boards use little or no big betting. Some paired or high-card boards still support frequent small bets.

## [17:15] Transition to low-EV board deep dives

The report-level strategy establishes which boards are good, neutral or poor for OOP before individual low-board solutions are studied.

## [18:05] Start with the lowest-EV boards

Nick recommends studying the most difficult boards first because they force OOP to build a heavy-check strategy and then defend that passive branch through delayed c-bets and check-raises.

The important skill is not only checking correctly. OOP must construct a meaningful check-raise range after IP stabs, especially because live opponents often fail to put enough money in when OOP only check-calls.

## [19:10] Linear check-raises against soft or over-stabbing opponents

In softer cash games, OOP can check-raise thin value more linearly when opponents:

- stab too frequently after being checked to;
- call and go to showdown without punishing a value-heavy raise range;
- fail to adjust to repeated linear pressure.

When IP stabs more, OOP should raise more. When IP checks back too much, OOP should take initiative more often itself.

## [20:30] Simple study sizes reveal the branch shape

The study gives IP a small and large stab size. If a board mixes both evenly, a single medium size may be sufficient in practice. If the report strongly prefers one branch, the board calls for a clearly small or large strategy.

Nick explicitly says this is a study method, not a mandatory in-game blueprint.

## [22:00] Why some high-card boards generate heavy checking from IP

On boards where IP's pocket pairs and second pairs can reach showdown without protection, IP lacks incentive to bet frequently. Top pair, ace-high and underpairs can check, while only selected low pairs or backdoors become bluffs.

These textures often coincide with boards that are relatively good for OOP.

## [23:20] Boards where IP bets most often

IP bets heavily on boards that are poor for OOP, including low paired or spread low boards where IP retains set and pair advantages. The predominant size is often small with some larger betting.

## [24:30] Build OOP's response from thin value plus supporting bluffs

On low-EV boards such as queen-low or jack-low spread textures, OOP should expect substantial IP aggression and build check-raises from:

- selected top pair and thin linear value;
- draws;
- backdoor candidates;
- occasional bottom-pair classes supporting the set region.

Exact combo choices remain visual-dependent.

## [25:30] Population frequency may be wrong in either direction

IP may over-stab some middling boards while under-stabbing the very low boards that require aggressive high-card play. The report supplies the baseline needed to identify which error is occurring before OOP adjusts.
