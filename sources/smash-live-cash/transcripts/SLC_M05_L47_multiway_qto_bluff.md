# Detailed Source-Faithful Record

Brad flats QTo on the BTN after an early-position open in an ante game, partly because the opener appears reluctant to play large pots. Nick treats that live context as evidence but warns against using one observation to justify a large range deviation.

Even a tight early-position player can open pocket pairs. The hand should not delete sets merely because the opener looked cautious. BTN’s cold-call range is dominated by pocket pairs, strong offsuit broadways and suited connected hands; those high-weight classes matter more than a few marginal suited additions.

On a three-way Q-5-3 two-tone flop, BTN bets top pair for value/protection; BB folds and the opener check-raises. The response must account for sets, flush draws and plausible bluffs rather than rely on the read alone. The multiway action and raise rapidly filter the range.

The main coaching issue is the loose preflop entry. If a player expands this far, the entire cold-call architecture should expand coherently—especially more playable suited Kx and connected classes—rather than adding QTo as a random exception.
