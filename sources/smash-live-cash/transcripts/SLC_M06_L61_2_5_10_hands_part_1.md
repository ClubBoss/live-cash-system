## [04:00] Squeeze Size and Range Shape

Open-plus-caller configurations should be squeezed aggressively with a linear good-hand region. Undersized squeezes give multiple players attractive prices and fail to generate the fold equity that makes the play profitable.

## [08:00] Postflop After the Multiway Squeeze

In low-SPR multiway pots, the aggressor must still account for ranges created by loose calls. Betting merely because the board is “bricky” is not enough; the line should target specific high-card and pair regions.

## [13:00] Small River Value

A tiny river bet can be mandatory when the opponent holds many one-pair hands that will call and the bettor has few natural bluffs. Fear of appearing unbalanced should not eliminate straightforward thin value.

## [18:00] Do Not Trap AA in an Obvious Squeeze Spot

A player flats AA after an open and calls, hoping someone behind will squeeze. Nick argues that the available pot and likely calls make direct squeezing superior. If an opponent squeezes too much, use hands that can profitably call and back-jam rather than wasting AA as the trap.

## [23:00] Squeeze More in Straddled Live Games

The course repeatedly recommends aggressive squeezing over opens and calls. Good hands print through fold equity and by isolating weaker ranges, in position or out of position.
