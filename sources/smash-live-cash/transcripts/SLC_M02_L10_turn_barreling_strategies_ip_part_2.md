# Detailed Transcript

## [00:00] Build Turn Barrels from Value First

Nick continues the in-position barreling framework on K-7-2 two-tone at 200bb. He first identifies the equilibrium flop response and which calls or check-raises live opponents are likely to miss.

## [01:55] Deep-Stack Selectivity

A slightly tighter flop range does not automatically justify indiscriminate turn aggression. At 200bb, one-pair value thresholds remain high and low-equity barrels must be chosen selectively.

## [03:50] How Flop Check-Raises Shape the Turn

Live players often check-raise fewer linear top-pair hands and fewer corresponding bluffs. Low nut-flush draws are important theoretical raises because they clear dominating ace-highs; missing them changes the turn range.

## [05:45] Paired Turn and Polar Overbet Tree

The turn pairs the deuce. IP has large sizes including 125% and 200% pot. Value is concentrated in full houses, trips and selected strong Kx rather than every top pair.

## [07:40] Match Bluff Volume to the Value Threshold

Because the value range is selective, the bluff range must also be selective. Nick organizes candidates into equity-driven, blocker-driven and very low-equity “savage” barrels.

## [09:35] Equity-Driven and Future-Runout Bluffs

Nut-flush draws and connected club hands can value-bet when they improve and supply bluffs on future club runouts. The chosen turn range must already contain the river candidates the strategy will need.

## [11:30] Blocker-Driven Candidates

Broadway gutters and single-club hands are selected partly for blocking Kx and strong continues, while weaker nut-flush blockers provide club-river bluffs. Showdown-heavy Ax is less attractive.

## [13:25] Low-Equity Barrels

Low offsuit Ax can be preferred because it blocks stronger Ax or A2, does not block target folds, and still has an overcard. These are difficult but necessary candidates when the population over-folds the turn.

## [15:20] Expected Live Over-Folds

Against a 200% pot bet, equilibrium continues some middle pairs without a club. Nick expects many live players to release 88-JJ too frequently, creating immediate turn EV.

## [17:15] River Follow-Through on a Brick

After the huge turn bet, river bluffing is selective. Some low Ax and a limited set of broadway candidates continue; flush-draw blockers can be neutral when OOP no longer has many flush draws.

## [19:10] Value Sizing and Bluff Support

The polarized range may use pot, 2x pot or 3x pot according to the value class. The bluff count must support that size rather than be chosen independently.

## [21:05] Exploit Adjustment

Versus players who over-fold middle pairs on the turn and top pair on the river, Nick recommends over-bluffing the turn with the correct low-equity candidates, then adjusting river follow-through to the actual remaining range.
