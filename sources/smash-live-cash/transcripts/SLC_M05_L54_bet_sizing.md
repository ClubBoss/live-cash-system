# Detailed Source-Faithful Record

The lesson focuses on replacing habitual sizes with a range-and-target process. A T-6-2-type flop in a roughly 100bb 3-bet pot creates a polar distribution: overpairs are far ahead while much of the high-card region remains weak and future cards change value relatively little.

Nick works backward from the river value target. A small flop bet can leave an awkward river stack and under-realise the strongest hands. A larger geometric size can build toward a river shove while pressuring QQ/JJ, ace-high and other bluff-catchers.

Large betting still requires a value region capable of stacking off and bluffs that block the strongest continues or retain equity. The board label alone does not license the size.

When the solver mixes nearby sizes with the same hand classes, one practical size can replace them as long as it preserves the intended river geometry.
