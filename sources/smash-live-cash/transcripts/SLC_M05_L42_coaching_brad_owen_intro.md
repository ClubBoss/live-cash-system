## [00:00] Purpose of the Brad Owen section

Nick introduces a set of lessons produced while coaching Brad Owen. Brad entered the work with extensive live-cash experience, strong intuition for table dynamics and player ranges, but very little solver or formal technical study.

## [00:40] Preserve the player's existing strengths

The coaching goal was not to replace Brad's feel-based game with a rigid solver imitation. His preflop understanding already captured the broad shapes of ranges and his live instincts identified when players wanted or did not want to play large pots.

The technical work was intended to give those instincts a stable baseline.

## [01:15] Charts are reference shapes, not exact scripts

Nick repeats the course's preflop philosophy:

- know where hand classes belong;
- understand the baseline shape;
- deviate deliberately in the moment;
- do not treat every mixed frequency as a memorisation requirement.

## [01:30] Brad's questions improved the exploitative framework

Brad repeatedly challenged purely range-based explanations with practical questions such as why a strong value hand should care which hands fold. That pushed the coaching toward more opponent-specific sizing and hand-class decisions.

Nick describes this as a useful correction to overly technical thinking: live strategy can use different sizes or lines for value and bluffs when the opponent will not punish the imbalance.

## [02:20] Theory plus hand-history review

Sessions combined:

- equilibrium baselines;
- deep hand-history review;
- discussion of the optimal branch;
- immediate adaptation to the actual opponent and live environment.

The recorded lessons usually take one hand or board and explore it deeply rather than surveying many shallow examples.

## [03:20] One spot should teach many spots

Nick prefers extracting mechanisms that transfer to ten, twenty or effectively unlimited related situations. The aim is to understand why ranges and actions interact, not to accumulate disconnected solver screenshots.

## [04:00] Technical improvement without losing intuition

Brad's technical understanding improved while his feel for the game remained intact. Nick explicitly warns against moving from one extreme to the other:

- intuitive but ungrounded play;
- rigid solver imitation that ignores image, timing, mood and live reads.

## [04:30] Image, mood and behavioural context remain strategic inputs

Live poker sometimes rewards actions taken for image, a strong read or a belief that an opponent wants to play a large pot. Nick says Brad helped him recognise that he had underweighted those factors in his own technical approach.

These inputs do not replace the baseline. They determine how to deviate from it.

## [05:10] The full live-cash package

The stated goal is:

1. understand equilibrium and optimal mechanics;
2. deviate in every profitable direction;
3. avoid opening an obvious counter-exploit;
4. incorporate tendencies, mood, history and unique live conditions.

## [05:45] Conclusion

Nick characterises the ideal style as intuitive and feel-based but not unstructured. The player knows the baseline used to make each deviation and uses all available information about the opponent and environment.
