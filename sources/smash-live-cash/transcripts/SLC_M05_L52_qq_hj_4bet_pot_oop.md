# Detailed Source-Faithful Record

Brad opens QQ in HJ in a deep straddled game and faces an opponent known to 3-bet very wide suited hands. The best adjustment is primarily to expand value, not merely add more bluffs.

Even a wild 4-bet-call range remains a strong node. The opponent can add suited gappers and connectors, but modelling should preserve playability rather than turn the range into random offsuit trash.

With a third blind, normal HJ behaves like an earlier position. Both baseline ranges should tighten before applying the opponent-specific expansion. Hero therefore reaches postflop with more linear value; Villain reaches with more low suited hands.

As the board develops, QQ can become a poor bluff-catcher if it blocks the opponent’s missed draws or fails to block value. Villain later claims a suited-gapper bluff, but being shown a bluff does not prove the fold wrong: a disciplined strategy must sometimes fold the winning hand.

# Explicit Instructor Mechanisms

- Against a sticky wide 3-bettor, expand value before bluffs.
- Model loose ranges with plausible suited and playable classes.
- Translate positions when a straddle adds a third blind.
- Judge bluff-catchers by blockers and ancestry; a shown bluff does not invalidate the fold.
