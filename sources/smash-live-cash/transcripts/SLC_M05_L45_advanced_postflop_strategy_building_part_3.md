# Detailed Source-Faithful Record

Low boards such as 7-3-2 are often dynamic because many turns complete or create straights and shift nut ownership. When IP owns high-equity overpairs and nut advantage but future equity can move sharply, large or overbet sizing can outperform a habitual small c-bet.

Two-tone and rainbow versions can use sizes differently because the defender’s backdoor inventory changes. Value and bluffs must be built together: overbet value comes from hands willing to play a large pot, while bluff candidates are chosen by blockers, future turns and the defender’s strongest continues.

On rivers, lower missed draws and no-showdown hands can become better bluffs when they avoid removing folds. A solver may compensate for missed earlier aggression with an extreme river size; humans often cannot reconstruct that range, turning an earlier simplification into a later leak.

# Explicit Instructor Mechanisms

- Low does not mean static; classify future runouts.
- Use large sizes when value and future equity shape preserve polarization.
- Do not let simplification destroy later bluff inventory.
- Adapt size to the opponent only after preserving the strategic job.
