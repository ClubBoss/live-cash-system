## [03:00] Exploring the BB Lead

BB has mixed leads, but no hand is forced to lead. Because mixed actions have similar EV, a player may simplify to pure checking unless an opponent-specific response makes the lead more profitable.

## [06:00] When an Optional Lead Becomes an Exploit

A lead gains value if opponents under-raise, overfold or fail to put money in after checks. Strong value may also lead more when BTN will not stab often enough to build the pot.

## [10:00] HJ Must Show Discipline

The initial opener has many hands with poor board interaction and must avoid treating preflop initiative as permission to c-bet. Both opponents retain nutted combinations and positional leverage.

## [14:00] BTN's Aggressive Stabs

After two checks, BTN can bet a wide range, including unintuitive low-equity hands. HJ is sandwiched and BB retains strong check-raises, making the stab effective even when it does not generate an immediate heads-up pot.

## [18:00] Later-Street Pressure

BTN can continue barreling when the turn does not restore HJ's range advantage. Removal against the check-call ranges becomes more important than visible flop equity.

## [22:00] Population Adjustment

Many live players miss the required BTN aggression and BB check-raises. This changes the value of checking strong hands and creates room for more direct value betting or leading.

## [26:00] Practical Framework

First assign nut advantage, then identify the sandwich, then decide whether the population will provide the expected aggression. Mixed solver leads are optional; population mistakes determine whether they become useful.
