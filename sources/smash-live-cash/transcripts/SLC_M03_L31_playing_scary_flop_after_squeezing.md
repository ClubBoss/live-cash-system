## Squeeze-pot leverage and range composition

Live squeeze pots contain dead money and narrow, high-leverage ranges. The example uses a deep BTN squeeze after an early-position open and CO call, followed by a CO call, on a low connected 9-6-5-type two-tone flop.

Before judging the board, Nick compares the high-weight preflop classes. The squeezer retains overpairs, AK/AQ and selected suited connectors; the caller is pair- and suited-hand-heavy with fewer premiums. A board that looks “scary” is therefore not automatically bad for IP.

## Small c-bet and difficult OOP defence

The baseline uses a frequent small c-bet. Correct OOP defence requires more than obvious value and draws: low-card matchers, backdoors and selected low-equity check-raises protect the value region.

When OOP omits those classes, IP can move toward a much higher c-bet frequency. The actionable lock is the missing hand class, not a label such as passive.

## Resistance filters the range

After a check-raise, IP must release some overpairs and top-pair classes without relevant backdoors. Continuing because the hand began strong ignores the range created by the raise-call node.

If OOP fails to seed flop bluffs, later turn and river branches become value-heavy. IP can then make disciplined folds even versus modest later sizes.

# Explicit Instructor Mechanisms

- Start with preflop range composition before calling a low board scary.

- Missing flop bluff inventory produces bluff-deficient later streets.
- Recalculate continuing thresholds after resistance.
