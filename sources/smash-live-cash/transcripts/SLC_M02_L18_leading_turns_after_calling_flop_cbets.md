# Detailed Transcript

## [00:00] Why Build Turn Leads?

After check-calling the flop, OOP can lead turns to force value into the pot, deny free cards and pressure unpaired hands that struggle to defend.

## [01:55] Three Families of Lead Spots

Nick lists: low turns after IP's high-card-heavy c-bet; paired second/bottom-pair turns after a polar big bet; and paired-board turns where vulnerable top pair can value/protection bet.

## [03:50] Three Example Boards

The examples are 9-5-4 two-tone, J-8-3 rainbow and K-Q-2 two-tone, all around 200bb.

## [05:45] 9-5-4: IP's Polar C-Bet

HJ's big c-bet contains overpairs plus high-card and draw bluffs. BB mostly calls rather than raises, preserving top pair and many draws.

## [07:40] Low Turns Improve OOP's Relative Position

On low cards that miss IP's high cards, BB's equity rises and leading becomes available. Paired 4 or 5 turns and straight-completing cards are especially favorable.

## [09:35] Lead Value and Bluff Construction

Top pair, two pair, straights and selected draws lead. Low cards and draws that avoid blocking IP's air support the bluff side.

## [11:30] Defence Against the Lead

IP is supposed to call and raise with difficult high-card and pair categories. Nick expects many live players to miss these responses.

## [14:38] J-8-3: Linear Flop Bet Produces Linear Small Leads

After a wide small c-bet and call, BB retains many Jx and 8x. When the 8 or 3 pairs, BB can lead small with trips, top pair, second pair and protection hands.
