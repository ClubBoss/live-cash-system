## [02:00] Monotone 3-Bet Pot

A deep monotone board creates a cooler-like overpair-versus-flush interaction, but the analysis still separates sets, straights, lower flushes and nut-card holdings rather than treating every strong one-pair hand identically.

## [05:00] River Value and Available Calls

Thin value must be supported by concrete worse calls. On a river that completes many natural bluffs, some hands can value-bet more confidently; on other lines the check-jam range becomes very value-heavy.

## [08:00] Underbluffed Aggressive Lines

Nick repeatedly asks what low-equity hands can realistically arrive and bluff. If the candidate region requires difficult pocket-pair or suited-connector conversions, many live players will not find it.

## [11:00] Second Hand and Stack-Off Discipline

The lesson examines whether a strong made hand should play for stacks when the opponent's check-jam range contains flushes and very few natural bluffs. Blocking one nutted region does not remove sets, straights or lower value hands.

## [14:00] Practical River Decision

When the value range is concrete and the bluff region is difficult to construct, folding a visually strong hand can be correct. Conversely, when holding the relevant nutted hand, direct value play is preferred over inducing aggression that may never occur.
