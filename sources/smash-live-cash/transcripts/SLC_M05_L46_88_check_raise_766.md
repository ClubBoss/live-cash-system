# Detailed Source-Faithful Record

In a straddled Aria game, Brad defends 88 and faces a relatively small flop bet on a paired 7-6-6 rainbow board.

88 can check-raise as a linear value/protection hand against a small, wide c-bet. It denies equity to overcards, remains ahead of many continues and keeps the raise branch from containing only trips and bluffs.

After the raise is called, both ranges are filtered. The main coaching criticism is putting too much money into the turn without recalculating the caller’s surviving value and bluff-catcher classes.

By the river, 88 can block natural bluff classes and arrive in a narrow range. The final fold is evaluated independently from the good flop raise: a correct early action does not justify a later call.

# Explicit Instructor Mechanisms

- Vulnerable overpairs can enter a linear check-raise range versus small wide bets.
- Recalculate both ranges after raise-call before investing heavily on the turn.
- Evaluate each street independently; a good flop action does not license a river call.
