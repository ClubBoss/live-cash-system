## [00:00] Question: when should top pair check-raise?

Nick introduces a board-comparison method rather than one universal rule. Some textures raise weak top pair heavily, some prefer high kickers and others keep top pair mainly in check-call.

## [01:55] Paired boards permit linear top-pair raises

When OOP retains trips, the range can support linear check-raises with top pair plus backdoor bluffs. The smaller raise size is often sufficient to combine value, protection and denial.

## [03:50] Dynamic future streets

Top pair cannot be treated as a static bluff-catcher. The range must preserve strong calls, raises and future-street size options so that difficult turns do not make OOP face-up.

## [05:45] Global board report

A report across representative flops identifies high, medium and low top-pair check-raise environments. The purpose is to learn repeating mechanics rather than memorise each matrix.

## [07:40] Vulnerable top pair and paired low boards

On boards such as J-4-4, J-2-2 and 9-3-3, vulnerable or low-kicker top pair can raise because calling is uncomfortable and the hand benefits from protection.

## [09:35] Boards where top pair remains passive

On more polar ace-high or connected boards, OOP may reserve raises for sets, two pair, draws and backdoors while using top pair as a value-catcher.

## [11:30] Low kicker clears more high cards

A low kicker can benefit more from raising because it does not block high-card c-bets that the raise is trying to fold. Stronger kickers can remain calls and dominate the betting range.

## [14:40] The 9-3-3 example: blocker and catch-up logic

On the demonstrated paired board, IP's air is concentrated in broadway and ten-plus high-card regions. Top pairs containing those cards—such as stronger T/J/Q/K kickers—often prefer calling because they block the air and can allow IP to pair a dominated kicker on later streets.

By contrast, lower-kicker 9x does not block those high-card bluffs and receives little benefit from allowing low cards to pair. It therefore check-raises more aggressively for protection and value.

## [16:00] Linear protection rather than a purely polar range

The check-raise range contains substantial medium-strength value rather than only trips and bluffs. The source describes this as a linear protection strategy: low-kicker top pair can force folds from high-card air and gain value from difficult continues.

Whether the exploit overperforms depends on which opponents can continue correctly with queen-high, king-high, ace-high and backdoor categories.

## [17:20] Building the non-value side

The aggressive range is supported by suited high-card and backdoor candidates with roughly medium equity. Ace-high and hands such as suited QJ/KT-type backdoors can mix into raises because OOP's passive check-call line performs poorly and the hands retain turn playability.

## [18:40] Final practical summary

When the board supports a high check-raise frequency:

- move selected comfortable calls into raises;
- prefer vulnerable top pair that unblocks IP's air;
- use suited backdoors and high-card candidates to support the linear value region;
- preserve stronger kicker calls that can dominate future pairs.
