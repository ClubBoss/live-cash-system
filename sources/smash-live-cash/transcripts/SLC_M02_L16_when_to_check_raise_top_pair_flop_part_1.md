## [00:00] Question: When Should Top Pair Check-Raise?

Nick introduces a board-comparison method rather than a single universal rule. Some textures raise weak top pair heavily, some prefer high kickers, and others keep top pair in check-call mode.

## [01:55] Paired Boards Permit Linear Top-Pair Raises

When OOP retains trips, the range can support linear check-raises with top pair plus backdoor bluffs. The smallest raise size is often used.

## [03:50] Dynamic Future Streets

Top pair cannot be treated as a static bluff-catcher. The range must mix strong checks, raises and different future-street sizes so that difficult turns do not make OOP face-up.

## [05:45] Global Board Report

## [07:40] Vulnerable Top Pair and Paired Low Boards

On boards such as J-4-4, J-2-2 and 9-3-3, vulnerable or low-kicker top pair often raises because calling is difficult and the hand benefits from protection.

## [09:35] Boards Where Top Pair Stays Passive

On more polar ace-high or connected boards, OOP may reserve raises for sets, two pair, draws and backdoors while using top pair as a value-catcher.

## [11:30] Low Kicker Clears More High Cards

On paired boards, a low kicker can benefit more from raising because it does not block the high-card c-bets the raise is trying to fold. Stronger kickers can remain calls and dominate the betting range.
