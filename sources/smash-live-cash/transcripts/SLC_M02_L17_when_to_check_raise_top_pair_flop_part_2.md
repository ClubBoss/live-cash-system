# Detailed Transcript

## [00:00] J-4-4: Punish Wide C-Bets with Vulnerable Top Pair

Low and medium Jx can raise heavily because calling is difficult and IP c-bets widely. Stronger kickers that dominate bluffs can call more often.

## [01:55] Backdoors and Low Cards Support the Range

Low Ax, backdoor Kx/Qx and connected low cards supply bluffs. Nick emphasizes using cards that do not block IP's weakest c-bets.

## [03:50] T-T-8 and Second-Pair Fast Plays

Against a near-range c-bet, OOP can raise Tx and selected 8x, including A8, to force money in and avoid later ambiguity.

## [05:45] 9-5-4 and Bet-Size Sensitivity

Low-kicker 9x and A9 can raise even versus a large c-bet. Against a smaller c-bet, frequency rises with the same general shape.

## [07:40] When Top Pair Should Not Raise

On some boards the c-bet is already narrow and strong. If a raise has little weak value to target, top pair stays in value-catcher mode and raises remain more polar.

## [09:35] Repeated Check-Raise Patterns

Sets, two pair, pair-plus-draw and high-card gutters recur as aggressive classes. The board comparison is intended to teach transferable shapes.

## [11:30] Human Adjustment: Fast-Play Value More Often

Nick expects live opponents to under-bluff later streets and fail to put enough money in after OOP slowplays. This can justify raising sets, strong draws and selected top pair more purely.

## [13:25] High-Card Dynamic Boards

On A-J-9 and A-Q-8 type boards, raise frequency depends strongly on c-bet size. Small bets invite more raises; large polar bets reduce the response.

## [15:20] Do Not Invent New Bluffs

When exploiting, overuse candidates already present in the baseline—flush draws, straight draws and correct backdoors—rather than digging for unrelated hands.

## [17:15] Large versus Small C-Bets

A large c-bet sharply reduces check-raise frequency and value thinness. A small c-bet permits broader protection raises and more semi-bluffs.

## [19:10] Population Passivity and Immediate Value

Nick prefers putting money in now with strong value or nut draws when opponents are unlikely to bluff or value-bet enough on later changing runouts.

## [21:05] Exploit by Weighting Existing Candidates

Keep the baseline value shape, then over-weight existing draws if opponents over-fold. Do not manufacture hands that the solver never considered.

## [23:00] Monotone Boards Stay Polar

On monotone boards OOP generally does not check-raise ordinary top pair. Raises concentrate in strong flushes, sets and low-card/backdoor hands needed for future paired or non-flush runouts.

## [24:55] Closing Pattern

The lesson closes by contrasting frequent top-pair raises on selected paired/dynamic boards with very polar monotone-board strategies.
