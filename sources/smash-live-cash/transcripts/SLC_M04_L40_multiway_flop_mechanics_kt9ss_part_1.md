## [03:00] Compare the Three Preflop Range Shapes

SB contains pairs, suited aces and suited connectors but lacks most premium hands and offsuit QJ. BB is wider and may retain more offsuit material, but also enters with many weak combinations. Live players may flat QJo and suited broadways too often, changing exact weights without necessarily removing HJ's advantage.

## [07:00] Why HJ Can C-Bet Aggressively

HJ's tight range, overpairs, sets, top pairs and nut straight density support high aggression. The opener can punish defenders whose preflop calls are too wide and insufficiently protected by 3-bets.

## [11:00] SB's Sandwich Constraint

SB acts between HJ and BB and cannot defend as freely as in a heads-up pot. Calls and check-raises must account for BB's remaining strong hands and HJ's uncapped range.

## [15:00] BB's Closing-Action Response

BB has greater freedom to call or check-raise after seeing SB's action. The correct range includes strong draws and removal-based bluffs, not only made hands.

## [20:00] Live Range Deviations

If SB and BB overcall preflop while missing the strongest squeeze hands, their ranges become wider and more capped. HJ can often increase aggression, but must still account for extra QJ or two-pair combinations that loose players retain.

## [25:00] Transferable Preflop-to-Flop Rule

Before selecting a multiway c-bet, compare which range owns the offsuit nut combinations, which premiums were removed by preflop action and which player is trapped in the sandwich.
