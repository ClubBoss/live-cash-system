## [00:00] Forcing the Wide Flop C-Bet

All right, so let's just quickly look at what happens when we force that wide c-bet. Again, it can be any kind of wide c-bet; it does not need to be the combo use that we talked about. The idea is that people have an exploit, they are tilted, or they are playing too aggressively for whatever reason—whether they have constructed a strategy that they think wins more money, or they are just in a mood that day or that moment.

What people will not do is have that exploit and then understand that they are supposed to check the flop 70–75% because they have way too much dust. What they will do is play it more normally. Look at how much our continue has changed: we do not have to fold all those hands that we were folding before. AQ is now continuing pure, AJ is continuing every time, all the suited broadways continue much more loosely, and there is much less mixing across many combos.

This probably goes even better for you in person. With that wide c-bet range, on a variety of turns they will have to shut down much more often with their air.

## [01:27] Why In-Position Can Continue Much Wider

This puts the concepts together. When the range is too tight, the out-of-position player has to draw bluffs from relatively strong hands. When the range is too loose, they cannot also draw heavily from those hands without over-bluffing. This is a spot where it is very easy for some opponents to over-bluff and barrel off.

Even if they do not over-bluff, versus the large size—the preferred polar size because they no longer have as much linear value—we are still continuing AK, AQ, and even some KQ because the opponent is so wide. That is versus a pot-sized bet, not versus a half-pot bet. It is a much looser continuation range than against the other locked profiles or even against the solid-pro range.

These spots are fluid, not rigid. You need to think about every opponent: what they do preflop, what that means postflop, and whether they actually compensate for their range. Most players will not. They will think, “I am the 3-bettor, I have aces and all the strong hands,” and keep blasting at least on the flop.

## [02:54] Turn and River Consequences of an Over-Wide Range

They may pump the brakes on the turn, but it is also easy for them to over-bluff because they can draw from more KQ, KJ, and other high-card material. They should slow down on many turns, but often they will not. On bricky runouts, even after large bets, the defender can end up calling down with high cards much more frequently because the wide range contains so many natural bluffs.

Pay close attention to the actual player. If they overdo it preflop, they probably overdo it on the flop. They may split on the turn, but either way it is often good to be sticky preflop and on the flop: the most likely tendency is either c-betting too often and continuing too aggressively, or c-betting too often and then giving up.

These are extreme profiles, apart from the solid-pro example, but the purpose of the extremes is to help us locate the correct adjustment somewhere in the middle and use our judgment against the specific player.
