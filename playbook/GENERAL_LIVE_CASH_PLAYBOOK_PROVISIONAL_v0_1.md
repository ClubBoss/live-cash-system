This document is not an exact chart set and does not replace range verification. It is a reasoning Playbook.

# The 16 provisional core rules

## Rule 1 — Start with pairwise effective stack

Before cards and ranges, identify:

- Hero versus each relevant opponent;
- stack in blinds or straddle units;
- likely SPR created by the preflop line.

Deeper:

- value position and suitedness more;
- protect flatting ranges more;
- tighten automatic stack-off thresholds.

Shorter:

- implied odds fall;
- high-card and top-pair value rise;
- commitment decisions become more direct.

**Cue:** `How deep is this exact confrontation?`

**Blocked:** exact 30–70bb thresholds require further source validation.

---

## Rule 2 — Expand exploits through existing candidates

When a table opens and over-calls too widely:

- squeeze more often with hands already plausible in the baseline;
- purify mixed candidates;
- do not manufacture random bluffs;
- retain protected flats when depth and aggressive players behind make them valuable.

**Cue:** `Candidate or invention?`

---

## Rule 3 — Identify the source range before reading the board

The same board can require opposite strategy depending on whether the caller is:

- BB;
- SB;
- a cold caller;
- a 3-bettor;
- the player closing multiway action.

Do not interpret board texture before asking which strong and weak combos reached it.

**Cue:** `Who arrived with which range?`

---

## Rule 4 — Every action filters the range

A flop call is not the original preflop range. A check-back is not the original flop range. A large bet and a small bet create different branches.

After every action ask:

- what folded;
- what raised;
- what remained;
- which strong hands were spent or preserved.

**Cue:** `What did this action remove?`

---

## Rule 5 — Deep OOP: protect the call range before forcing aggression

On dynamic boards at high SPR:

- check frequently;
- keep some overpairs, strong ace-highs and strong draws in call;
- use selective raises that target weak stabs and do not block folds;
- versus larger bets, generally call more selectively and raise less.

Do not raise merely to escape future OOP decisions.

**Cue:** `Does the range need this raise, or do I just hate the turn?`

---

## Rule 6 — Value threshold first, bluff volume second

Before bluffing:

1. define the weakest value hand for the intended size;
2. identify how much value exists;
3. select enough bluffs to support that value and size.

Do not choose a hand to bluff and then invent a story.

**Cue:** `What is my weakest value hand?`

---

## Rule 7 — Every barrel needs a future job

A non-value barrel should be one of:

- equity-driven;
- blocker/matcher-driven;
- low-showdown bluff that unblocks folds and covers missing river classes.

State:

- value rivers;
- bluff rivers;
- shutdown rivers.

**Cue:** `Which rivers does this hand cover?`

---

## Rule 8 — Large sizing requires preserved polarization

Overbet when:

- strong value remains concentrated in your range;
- Villain is capped or medium-strength heavy;
- the new card does not materially repair Villain's range;
- enough bluff supply exists.

A neutral low card can be a better overbet card than a visually scary ace or flush completer.

**Cue:** `Did this card preserve my advantage or repair theirs?`

---

## Rule 9 — Read the range behind the size

Small and large bets are not interchangeable.

Common shape:

- small/wide bet → more calls and linear raises required;
- large/polar bet → tighter continues and more polar raises;
- large but incorrectly value-heavy → fold speculative region aggressively;
- small but value-heavy → do not defend automatically because of price.

**Cue:** `How much weak range exists behind this size?`

---

## Rule 10 — Convert player labels into branch errors

Replace:

- nit;
- whale;
- passive;
- aggro;

with:

- which hands are missing from this bet;
- which hands appear too often;
- which hands are checked instead;
- which size contains medium-strength value;
- what evidence supports the claim.

Unknown branch returns to baseline.

**Cue:** `What exact hand class is added or missing?`

---

## Rule 11 — A 3-bet-pot range begins preflop

Before later-street defence, identify whether the 3-bettor actually contains:

- low suited connectors;
- suited one-gappers;
- middling pocket pairs;
- offsuit high-card value;
- natural broadway draws.

Missing preflop bluffs create missing river bluffs.

**Cue:** `Which bluff families entered preflop?`

---

## Rule 12 — Against value-heavy 3-bets, dominated big cards lose first

When the 3-bet range is concentrated in premiums:

- AQ/KQ-like hands can perform worse than their visual strength suggests;
- domination damages high-card realization;
- pocket pairs and selected low suited hands can retain relatively more value.

This is directional, not an exact call chart.

**Cue:** `Strong hand—or dominated hand against this range?`

---

## Rule 13 — Wide ranges must compensate by checking more

If Villain enters too wide and still c-bets normally:

- defend wider on the flop;
- observe whether the turn branch gives up or over-bluffs;
- do not assume a wide preflop error disappears after the flop.

**Cue:** `Did this weak range earn this betting frequency?`

---

## Rule 14 — Split strong bet branches from weak check branches

A player can:

- bet overpairs too often;
- check too many high cards;
- therefore have a strong flop-bet range and a weak check-back range.

Adjustment:

- respect the bet;
- attack suitable turns after the check.

**Cue:** `Which strong hands were already spent?`

---

## Rule 15 — Multiway defence is shared; aggression needs backup

In multiway pots:

- Hero does not carry the full defence burden;
- the sandwiched player tightens;
- the closing player has more freedom;
- bluffs require equity, removal and credible continuation;
- random heads-up air is usually removed;
- top pair without backup equity can become fragile.

**Cue:** `Who can still wake up behind me?`

---

## Rule 16 — Trace value and bluffs before trusting a blocker

Before a major river call, fold or bluff:

1. list the value hands that reached the node;
2. remove value inconsistent with the size;
3. list actual bluff families;
4. trace them back through prior streets;
5. evaluate what Hero's blocker removes from value, bluffs and folds.

A visible nut blocker alone is not a reason to act.

**Cue:** `What reached this node, and what does my card truly remove?`

# Four compact decision algorithms

## Single-raised pot

`NODE → SIZE SHAPE → RANGE FILTER → CARD OWNERSHIP → COMBO JOB → RIVER PLAN`

1. Positions, depth, blind identity.
2. Small/wide, large/polar or unusual branch.
3. Update after bet/call/check.
4. Who benefits from the new card?
5. Value, protected call, equity bluff, blocker bluff or reject.
6. Continue, change size or shut down by river class.

## 3-bet pot

`PREFLOP SHAPE → BLUFF SUPPLY → FLOP BRANCH → COMPENSATION → FUTURE BLUFFS`

1. Polar, linear, value-heavy or over-wide.
2. Which low suited and high-card bluffs exist?
3. Bet branch or check branch?
4. Did Villain compensate for preflop deviation?
5. Which bluffs survive to later streets?

## Multiway pot

`NUT OWNER → SANDWICH → SHARED DEFENCE → EXPECTED AGGRESSION → BACKUP EQUITY`

1. Who owns offsuit nuts and premiums?
2. Who acts between live ranges?
3. Who closes action?
4. Will population produce solver-required raises/stabs?
5. Does Hero have equity and removal if action continues?

## River bluff-catcher

`VALUE → SIZE EXCLUSIONS → BLUFF ANCESTRY → HERO BLOCKERS → EVIDENCE`

No call or fold is finalized before completing this order.

# Fast table checklist

Before a difficult decision, ask only the relevant questions:

1. Effective stack?
2. Heads-up or multiway?
3. Which range arrived?
4. What does the size represent?
5. What did prior action remove?
6. Who owns this card?
7. What is my hand's job?
8. What happens on the next street?
9. Is the exploit branch-specific and evidenced?

# Initial opponent overlays

## Value-heavy 3-bettor

- defend the 3-bet tighter;
- remove dominated high cards first;
- demand proof of later bluffs.

## Over-wide 3-bettor who c-bets normally

- continue flop wider;
- classify turn as give-up or over-barrel branch.

## Overpair fast-player

- tight versus bet;
- pressure after check-back.

## Flop over-folder / under-check-raiser

- bet more flop;
- update to stronger range after call.

## Excessive small c-bettor

- call and raise more linearly with suitable high-card/backdoor region.

## Passive multiway player

- fast-play value;
- do not wait for aggression that will not arrive.

## River under-bluffer

- trace missing bluff supply;
- make disciplined large folds.

## Aggressive blocker bluffer

- count narrow value and size exclusions before folding.
