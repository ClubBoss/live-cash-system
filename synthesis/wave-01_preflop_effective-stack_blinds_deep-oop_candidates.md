# Candidate heuristics

## H-W01-001 — Effective stack sets the preflop architecture

**Status:** `SOURCE-SUPPORTED / PROVISIONAL`  
**Confidence:** medium-high

### Trigger

Before choosing a preflop line, especially versus a 3-bet, 4-bet, squeeze or straddle.

### Default

Classify the hand by effective stack, not by the number of chips in front of Hero.

- Around 100bb: more direct 3-bet/get-in structures and fewer protected flats.
- Around 200bb+: more strong flats, more calls versus 4-bets and tighter stack-off thresholds.
- At very deep stacks: value position, suitedness and postflop playability more; enter large preflop pots selectively.

### Why

`SLC-M01-L03` explicitly contrasts 100bb, 200bb and 400bb structures. `SLC-M01-L02` also states that depth increases the value of protected flats and postflop playability.

### Main exception

Exact boundaries and hand classes remain visual-dependent. Do not turn this mechanism into an improvised chart.

### Table cue

`What is the effective stack, and does this hand want a low-SPR or high-SPR pot?`

### Drill

Given ten preflop spots, state only:

1. effective stack;
2. direct-value / protected-flat / playable-call category;
3. what changes if depth moves from 100bb to 200bb.

No exact frequencies required.

## H-W01-002 — Expand squeezes by purifying existing candidates

**Status:** `SOURCE-SUPPORTED / PROVISIONAL`  
**Confidence:** high on the mechanism

### Trigger

Open plus one or more callers, especially when the opener and callers are too wide and over-call squeezes with weak ranges.

### Default

Increase squeeze frequency with hands already present as baseline mixed candidates. Examples named in the lesson include strong offsuit broadways, suited broadways and suited connectors.

Do not add random hands merely because the table appears weak.

### Why

`SLC-M01-L02` says live squeezing is underused because opens and calls are too wide, and recommends purifying existing mixed candidates rather than inventing arbitrary bluffs.

### Main exception

At deeper stacks, retain some strong flats to protect the calling range and exploit aggressive players behind. The exploit is not `always squeeze every strong hand`.

### Table cue

`Is this already a plausible squeeze candidate, or am I manufacturing action?`

### Drill

For each open-plus-call spot, classify candidate hands into:

- pure value squeeze;
- baseline mixed candidate that can be purified;
- protected flat;
- fold.

The drill should test category logic, not chart memory.

## H-W01-003 — Straddle changes the denominator; preserve SPR

**Status:** `SOURCE-SUPPORTED + SYSTEM INFERENCE / PROVISIONAL`  
**Confidence:** medium-high

### Trigger

Any live pot with a straddle.

### Default

- Recalculate effective depth in straddle units before acting. `SYSTEM INFERENCE` from the source's depth and straddle framework.
- Do not mechanically use oversized 4x opens when they destroy useful SPR.
- Small blind first-in should use an aggressive raise strategy rather than passive limping.
- Outside the straddle, cold-call more selectively; the straddle retains the widest calling range.

### Why

`SLC-M01-L03` explicitly warns against automatic oversized opens in straddled pots, recommends preserving SPR, and distinguishes SB and straddle blind behaviour.

### Main exception

The source does not yet verify one universal live opening size. Table rake, number of limpers, player elasticity and actual effective depth can change the best size.

### Table cue

`How many straddles deep are we, and what SPR does this raise create?`

### Drill

Translate nominal stacks into straddle units:

- 200bb in a 2x straddle structure;
- 150bb against a 60bb player;
- multiway with different pairwise effective stacks.

Then state whether the chosen raise creates a shallow, medium or deep postflop environment.

## H-W01-004 — Identify the blind range before reading the board

**Status:** `SOURCE-SUPPORTED / PROVISIONAL`  
**Confidence:** high on the mechanism

### Trigger

Single-raised pot where a blind called preflop.

### Default

Before applying a familiar board heuristic, identify whether the caller is BB or SB.

- BB often reaches the flop wider and less condensed.
- SB reaches with a tighter, stronger pocket-pair, suited and broadway structure.
- A board that supports near-range c-betting versus BB may require heavy checking versus SB.

### Why

`SLC-M02-L05` demonstrates that identical boards can produce opposite c-bet strategies because the source range differs.

### Main exception

Live players may use unusual SB cold-call or limp-call ranges. Replace the theoretical blind label with the actual observed range when evidence is strong.

### Table cue

`Which blind called, and what strong hands survive in that range?`

### Drill

Show the same five flops twice:

- opener versus BB;
- opener versus SB.

The learner must predict only `more aggressive`, `similar`, or `more checking`, then explain the range reason.

## H-W01-005 — After exploiting weak flop defence, update the turn range

**Status:** `SOURCE-SUPPORTED / PROVISIONAL`  
**Confidence:** high

### Trigger

You increased flop c-bet frequency because a blind over-folds or under-check-raises, and the opponent called.

### Default

Do not continue turn aggression as though the original weak range remained intact. The call filters out many weak hands and creates a stronger, more condensed turn range.

### Why

`SLC-M02-L06` separates the easy flop exploit from the later-street problem and explicitly notes that the calling range becomes stronger.

### Main exception

Some opponents call the flop far too wide as well as under-raise. That is a different node lock and must be supported by observation.

### Table cue

`My flop exploit worked often—but what does this particular call now represent?`

### Drill

For each weak-defence profile, compare:

- range before facing the flop bet;
- range after calling;
- which turn cards still justify pressure.

## H-W01-006 — Deep OOP on dynamic boards: protect the call range first

**Status:** `SOURCE-SUPPORTED / PROVISIONAL`  
**Confidence:** high

### Trigger

Deep single-raised pot, OOP, dynamic low or connected board, high SPR.

### Default

- Use substantial checking and calling.
- Keep some overpairs, strong ace-highs and strong draws in check-call.
- Use selective low-card and backdoor check-raises that do not block IP's weak stabs.
- Versus larger c-bets, call more and raise less.

### Why

`SLC-M02-L21` distinguishes raw equity from realized EV and explains that strong hands must protect the middle of the OOP calling range.

### Main exception

On static boards or against an opponent with extreme bet/fold tendencies, a more aggressive branch may be justified. That requires a separate opponent model.

### Table cue

`Am I raising because the range needs it, or because I dislike playing another street OOP?`

### Drill

Given a deep OOP flop and a c-bet size, choose among:

- protected check-call;
- selective check-raise;
- fold.

The answer must state what strong hands protect the call range and which weak stabs the raise targets.
